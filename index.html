Single-outing pitch counts vs. Pitch Smart

Each bar is one outing. Reference lines are USA Baseball Pitch Smart rest thresholds for ages 11–12.
Workload note (informational, not medical advice). USA Baseball Pitch Smart caps ages 11–12 at 85 pitches/day, with rest tied to count: 66+ → 4 days, 51–65 → 3 days, 36–50 → 2 days, 21–35 → 1 day. No outing here topped 85, and games were ~1 week apart so rest thresholds were comfortably met. The two highest single-day loads were G Nebo (72) and N Fujishiro (69) — both in the "4-day rest" band. On Oct 4 the high was F Lane (41), in the 2-day band, and on Oct 18 C Davis (63), in the 3-day band; on Oct 25 it was A Stanford (53) in the first game and N Fujishiro (55) in the second, both in the 3-day band; on Nov 7 it was G Balogh (42) in the first game and R Lovati (46) in the second, both in the 2-day band. Swing decisions

Whiff% = swings that missed. Hitters with at least 8 plate appearances; dashed line = team average.
misses less than the team near team average misses more than the team
How to read this. Swing% is swings per pitch seen. Whiff% is misses per swing. Called% is called strikes per pitch (strikes taken). 1st Sw% is how often the hitter swung at the first pitch. 2K counts plate appearances that reached two strikes and Surv% the share of those that did not end in a strikeout; Fl2 is fouls with two strikes. K-L and K-S are called and swinging third strikes (a dropped third strike counts as a strikeout either way).

Reading it. Avg BIP is hits per ball in play. Left / Mid / Right is where the ball went by the fielder who played it (left = third, short, left field; middle = pitcher, catcher, center; right = first, second, right field). Hard only counts balls the scorer marked as hard-hit, so it undercounts.
